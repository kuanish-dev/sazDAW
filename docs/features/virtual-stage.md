# Virtual stage: placing the channels in one room (`stage.*`)

Drag the instruments around a room and hear them move: left/right, ahead/behind, near/far and up/down, live while the song plays. The listener stands in the middle of the room; on headphones a sound placed behind or above them sounds behind or above.

* **Contract** (`daw-contracts/src/stage.rs`): `StageControl` — job "where each sound stands in the shared room": `room()`, `place(node, pan, StagePlace { depth, height })`, `take_off(node)`, `set_room(Room { size, reverb, listening })`; `MAX_ON_STAGE` = 64. The listener stands in the middle of the room facing ahead, and all three coordinates are 0 at their head: `pan` −1…1 wall to wall, `depth` −1…1 from the wall behind the listener to the wall ahead, `height` −1…1 floor to ceiling. A channel (a track's source or a bus — anything with a `Strip`) stands on the stage while `Strip::stage: Option<StagePlace>` is set (in JSON `stage: { depth, height }` inside the node kind; absent — off the stage, plain pan). Its left/right **is the strip's `pan`**, wall to wall, so the mixer's pan knob, pan automation and MIDI learn move it, and nothing duplicates it. `Project::room` is the one shared room (`size` 0…1 small studio room → concert hall, `reverb` 0…1 open air → live walls, `listening` `speakers`/`headphones`: what the stage is rendered for, saved with the project; two speakers in front cannot put a sound behind the listener, headphones can). Values are clamped; codes (area GR): `NO_STAGE_PLACE` GR-123 (an effect or the master), `NOT_ON_STAGE` GR-124 (a depth/height lane or MIDI mapping of a channel off the stage), `STAGE_NOT_A_NUMBER` GR-125, `TOO_MANY_ON_STAGE` GR-212. Consecutive `place` calls of one channel merge into one undo step (key `stage:<id>`, the knob window), so a drag is one step; so do `set_room` calls. `take_off` keeps the pan and removes the channel's depth/height lanes. Implementation `daw-project/src/stage.rs` (`ProjectStage`).
* **Why on the strip.** The place is a property of the channel, like pan. A built-in effect could not feed one shared room, and a "stage node" with positioned inputs would put the place on connections that every rewiring (inserting an effect, changing the output) would have to carry along. On the strip it stays with the channel through any rewiring, and the engine decides where in the path it acts.
* **Automation and MIDI learn.** `ParamTarget::StageDepth { node }` and `StageHeight { node }` (both −1…1); a lane is valid only while the channel stands on the stage (`ops::validate_target` through `ops::stage_spot`) and goes with it (`ops::drop_stage_lanes` on `take_off`, `drop_node`). The engine plays them as per-segment overrides like pan (`GraphMixer::override_stage_depth/height`). MIDI learn moves them through `StageControl::place` (`MidiMappingDeps::stage`); a stage drag reports pan/depth/height touches to live writing for a channel already on the stage.
* **Engine** (`daw-engine/src/stage/`: `geometry.rs` pure math, `hrtf.rs` the direction cue (the head and the outer ear), `voice.rs` one channel, `room.rs` the shared tail, `dsp.rs` the small filters and the delay line that refactor batch 6 moves to `daw-dsp`). The mixer (`compile.rs: bind_stage`) binds a voice to every placed channel — `MAX_ON_STAGE` preallocated slots keyed by node id, so a channel keeps its lines and its glide across snapshots, and one leaving gives its slot to one arriving in the same snapshot — and runs it **after the last node of the channel's insert path** (`insert_path_end`, the rule of `Graph::insert_path` and `mixerStrips.ts`): the track's effects shape the instrument's own sound first, and a compressor cannot undo the distance. It runs after `tap.output` (an insert's live picture shows its own output). The placed channel's strip applies its volume but not its pan. The room tail joins the master's input before its fader (`render_room`, so the master's picture and meter include it); only channels whose sound reaches the master feed it (`reaches_master`). The lines are allocated in `Stage::new`/`prepare` (`GraphMixer::prepare` runs when the stream opens, outside the callback); `process` allocates and locks nothing. The sound model:
  - *Geometry* (mirrored for the picture by `app/src/domain/stage.ts`): size → width 6–30 m, depth 5–24 m, height 3–10 m, ring time 0.35–2.8 s, wall lowpass 9–4.5 kHz (all geometric). The listener stands in the middle of the room, ears halfway up, facing ahead (+depth); every coordinate reaches to 0.5 m off its walls (`WALL_GAP`). Where a sound comes from is a `Direction` — the unit vector to it split into `lateral` (towards the right ear), `front` (along the nose) and `elevation` (up). Inside 0.5 m of the head (`NEAREST`) the vector shrinks to nothing and the level stops growing, so a source dragged through the listener passes from ahead to behind without a jump.
  - *Direct sound:* one point source (the middle of the channels). Level by the inverse distance law beyond 2 m (−6 dB per doubling; never louder than itself). Air: a one-pole lowpass from 20 kHz up close to about 5 kHz 25 m away. Then the direction cue below.
  - *Direction cue* (`hrtf.rs`) — on **headphones** a compact parametric HRTF instead of a measured set (no file, no allocation, fits most heads about equally well); on **speakers** only what two speakers in front can carry. Everything is tuned against straight ahead: a sound from straight ahead at ear height passes unchanged in both modes, so switching never changes a centred sound's tone. Two parts:
    - *The head* (`Head`, per ear, after the outer ear): on speakers, equal-power panning by `lateral` (×√2, so a centred source keeps its level). On headphones the far ear gets the interaural time difference (Woodworth's spherical head, (a/c)(θ + sin θ), up to ≈ 0.66 ms, a fractional delay), −1.9 dB from the side (`FAR_EAR_LEVEL`) and the head's shadow (Brown–Duda first-order shelf above ≈ 1.25 kHz, −14 dB highs from the side, `FAR_EAR_HIGHS`), and the near ear gets +3.5 dB highs (`NEAR_EAR_HIGHS`). All of it follows `lateral` alone, so it is the same ahead and behind (the cone of confusion, as with a real head).
    - *The outer ear* (`Pinna`: four biquads in a row on the source, before it splits into the ears): it tells ahead from behind and up from down. **Behind** (`rear` = how far `front` is below 0, headphones only): a high shelf −8 dB above 3 kHz (`REAR_HZ`/`REAR_DB`: the forward-facing pinna takes the presence and the air), +3 dB around 1.1 kHz (`REAR_BAND_*`, Blauert's rear directional band: a little body) and the notch below at 7.5 kHz — about −4 dB at 3 kHz, −12 dB at 6 kHz, −17 dB at 7.5 kHz, −8 dB at 16 kHz, within 0.5 dB under 500 Hz. **Height** (both modes): a ±4 dB shelf above 7 kHz (`LIFT_*`) and a notch (`NOTCH_*`, Q 2, up to −9 dB) that moves from 4.6 kHz straight below to 12 kHz straight above; its depth is the larger of |elevation| and `rear`, so it is gone straight ahead at ear height. On speakers `rear` is always 0: behind sounds like its mirror ahead, and the Stage view says so.
    - *How to tune it:* every number is a named constant at the top of `hrtf.rs` with what it does. Stronger or weaker front/back: `REAR_DB` and `REAR_BAND_DB` (the main cue; much beyond −12 dB makes rear sources sound muffled rather than behind). A brighter or darker overall rear tone: `REAR_HZ`. The height impression: `NOTCH_OCTAVES` (how far the notch travels) and `LIFT_DB`. Lateral width on headphones: `FAR_EAR_LEVEL`/`FAR_EAR_HIGHS`. How fast the tone may follow a move: `PINNA_SLEW_SECONDS` in `voice.rs`. Check a change by ear on headphones with a bright source (hi-hats, noise) dragged around the head, then against `stage/tests.rs` (`the_outer_ear_is_flat_ahead_darker_behind_and_moves_its_notch_with_height` pins the shapes, `headphones_tell_behind_from_ahead_and_speakers_cannot` the result through a voice). A measured HRTF set would replace `Pinna` and `Head::direct` with per-direction filters (FIR or fitted biquads) chosen from `Direction`; everything around them stays.
  - *Early reflections:* image sources in all six surfaces — the left and right walls, the walls ahead of and behind the listener, the floor and the ceiling (first order) — each later by its extra path (up to 120 ms), as loud as its path's inverse distance × wall reflectivity (0.8 × reverb), from its own direction and darkened by the walls. On speakers each is panned by its `lateral`. On headphones each reaches the far ear later by its own interaural delay, the far ear also hears it up to 6 dB quieter from the side (the head's shadow folded into its level, `REFLECTION_FAR_EAR`), and the share of it that comes from behind takes the outer ear's rear shelf (`rear_shade`, one per ear) — so a source behind the listener is heard behind in its echoes too (five of its six reflections come from behind). A source near a wall or high up hears that wall early and loud: depth and height are heard in the room's echo pattern, not only in level.
  - *Room tail* (`room.rs`): one feedback delay network of eight lines with a Hadamard mix, per-line damping and the feedback that rings for the ring time. Line lengths are fixed (the size changes how long and how dark it rings), so the size slider never clicks. Each channel feeds it its dry sound × 0.4 × reverb from the moment of its first reflection, leaning halfway to its side. Like a real reverberant field it does not fall with distance, so the direct-to-reverberant ratio is the main distance cue: at half reverb the room is as loud as the direct sound about 8 m away. It sleeps 1.5 ring times after its input stops.
  - *Smoothness:* the place glides (a 40 ms one-pole step per segment), and every per-sample value — gains, interaural delays, shadow, air, reflection delays, levels and rear shares, room send — ramps linearly across the segment, so dragging never clicks; a moving delay bends the pitch a little, like a real moving source. The outer ear's filters take new coefficients every 32 frames from the ramped cue, and the cue itself moves no faster than ahead → behind in 50 ms (`PINNA_SLEW_SECONDS`): a source passing right by the head turns around within a few milliseconds, and the filters' steps would click if the cue followed it.
  - *Not modelled:* head tracking (the sound turns with your head, so front/back confusions stay possible — they are with real binaural recordings too), a measured or personal HRTF (the parametric one is an average head), near-field cues (a source within arm's reach has a much larger level difference between the ears), the travel time of sound (a far instrument would play late), the width of a stereo instrument (it becomes one point), rooms other than a rectangle. Speakers stay plain stereo: a real "behind" there needs surround speakers.
* **RPC / MCP:** `stage.place { node, pan, depth, height }` (depth −1…1, negative behind the listener), `stage.takeOff { node }`, `stage.room`, `stage.setRoom { size, reverb, listening }` (`daw-rpc/src/stage.rs`; MCP tools `stage_*` follow from the table). `project.get` carries `room` and each strip's `stage`; `project.summary` shows `stage` with the track's channel. Renders include the stage: export, stems, and a bounced track (its audio has its place and share of the room baked in; the copy plays off the stage).
* **Frontend.** The "Stage" tab (`ui/stage/StageView.tsx`): the toolbar (room size with its measures, reflections, Speakers/Headphones, "Place all"), the room from above (`StageRoom.tsx`: the listener in the middle with shoulders and the nose pointing ahead, the line between the ears dashed with "ahead"/"behind you" at the walls, the stage area all around, a metre grid, rings of equal distance from 2 m where the direct sound starts to fall; on speakers the half behind is shaded and labelled "heard behind only on headphones" (`scene.listening`); a higher dot floats above its shadow), the side view (`StageSide.tsx`: behind ↔ ahead × height, the listener in the middle facing ahead, ear height dashed), the readout of the selected channel (distance, direct level, direction — "30° left" ahead, "behind, 30° left" beyond ±90°) with L/R, Depth and Height chips that open the parameter menu (automation lane, MIDI learn), and the channel list (`StageList.tsx`: Place / Take off, or drag a channel into the room). Each dot (`StageDot.tsx`) is in its track's color and glows with its meter (`session.levels`, like the track header). The Speakers button's hint explains that two speakers cannot put a sound behind you and that a true "behind" needs headphones or surround speakers. Dragging moves pan and depth (from above) or depth and height (from the side); the wheel sets the height, a double-click returns it to ear height, arrows and PageUp/PageDown move a focused dot. While a dot is dragged both pictures show the dragged place (`scene.ts`), so a re-read one move behind cannot pull it back. Actions `actions/stage.ts` (`StageActions`): `place` shows the place at once (`patchProject`) and sends one call at a time per channel, the latest place winning (`latestWins`), so the engine hears the move as the hand makes it; `placeAll` puts every off-stage channel in an arc (`domain/stage.ts: ensembleSpots`) in one undo step (`history.checkpoint`/`history.squash`, now also in `api.history`); `setRoom` goes the same way as `place`. Mock: `mock/handlers/stage.ts` (the same checks and codes), `MockWorld.edit(label, change, key)` merges keyed edits like the backend, `history.checkpoint/squash` in `mock/handlers/project.ts`; it plays no sound.
* **Tests:** contract — `daw-project/src/tests/stage.rs` (placing and taking off, clamping, codes, a drag is one undo step, the room, the 64 limit, depth/height lanes) and `tests/midi_mappings.rs` (a knob moves the depth); engine — `daw-engine/src/stage/tests.rs` (geometry with the listener in the middle, a source straight ahead keeps its level, direction, distance, reflections from behind for a source behind, the head is the same ahead and behind, the outer ear's shapes (flat ahead, darker with a notch behind, the notch moving with height), on headphones behind is darker than ahead while speakers cannot tell them apart, the interaural delay on headphones, a target jumping through the listener's head still moves without jumps, reflections and the room input from the first reflection, the tail's ring time and sleep, voice slots) and `daw-engine/src/tests/stage.rs` (a placed channel plays from its place, the stage acts after the inserts, the room rings on, a channel routed nowhere stays silent in the room, automation moves the depth); RPC — `daw-rpc/tests/all/stage.rs`.
* **How to extend.** A new cue (the travel time, a source width, second-order reflections): what the listener hears goes into `Cues` (`geometry.rs`, pure and testable), its per-sample value into `Mix` (ramped like the others), the work into `Voice::process`, any memory into `Voice::new`. A new direction cue (another pinna band, a near-field level difference, a measured HRTF): `hrtf.rs` — per-ear values into `Head`, filters on the source into `Pinna` (with its value in `PinnaCue`, ramped and slew-limited by the voice), a test of its shape in `stage/tests.rs`. A new room setting: a field of `Room` (with `Room::clamped`) → `Acoustics` / `RoomTail::set_shape` → the mock and the toolbar. A new automatable coordinate: a `ParamTarget` variant, `ops::validate_target`/`target_value`, an engine override, `domain/automation.ts` (label, range, value).
