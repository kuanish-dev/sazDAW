# Warping audio clips to tempo, pitch, and crossfades

**Model** (`daw-contracts/src/warp.rs`, `Clip` fields): `warp: Option<Warp { source_bpm }>` — the audio was recorded at `source_bpm` and is stretched by a factor of `source_bpm / tempo`, so a loop stays in beats; the length of such a clip in ticks does NOT depend on the tempo (it is computed in beats of the source tempo: `Clip::ticks_to_source_frames` / `source_frames_to_ticks` — the single formula, used by split, the rules, and the engine). `pitch_semitones` (±24, to hundredths) — pitch without changing length, both with and without warp. Processing: time-stretch by `stretch × 2^(s/12)` (WSOLA: ~40 ms Hann windows, the window offset chosen by waveform similarity — coarsely on decimated mono, then precisely) and resample the frames back to `stretch` (cubic interpolation, with a low-pass filter when shrinking) — `daw-stretch/src/{wsola,resample}.rs`. 30 s of stereo takes ~0.2 s in release.

**Editing** (`ClipControl::edit_clip`, rules in `daw-project/src/warp_rules.rs`): `ClipEdit.warp` — `Some(Some(WarpEdit { source_bpm }))` enables warp / changes the tempo (`None` — guess: a number with "bpm" in the file name, otherwise a standalone number 60–200 in the name, otherwise length = 1/2/4/8/16 bars of 4/4 at a tempo of 70–180, closest to 120; if nothing is guessed — the project tempo), `Some(None)` (JSON `null`) disables it. When warp changes, the clip keeps the same piece of audio: the length is recalculated; an explicit `lengthTicks` in the same edit takes precedence. RPC is the same `clips.edit`: `{ "warp": {} }` to guess, `{ "warp": { "sourceBpm": 128 } }`, `{ "warp": null }`, `{ "pitchSemitones": -2.5 }` (there is no separate `guessBpm`: guessing is part of enabling, and the guessed value is visible in the response). MCP sees this from the method description.

**Audio outside the audio thread.** Contract `ProcessedAudio` (`ready` — the finished result, or queue in the background without waiting; `compute` — with waiting, for export; `finished` — a counter of completed computations; `retain` — what is still needed). The key is `ProcessJob { sample, stretch (to 1e-6), pitch (to 0.01), curve? }`. Implementation `StretchCache` (`daw-stretch/src/cache.rs`): one background thread `daw-stretch`; a job that stopped being needed while waiting in the queue is not computed (moving the tempo quickly doesn't pile up work); a failure goes to the log (`source: "stretch"`), with no retries; memory — `retain` drops everything not in the latest snapshot. A stretch outside 0.25…4 is rejected (the sound would be ruined and memory would grow several-fold). The core (`service/sync.rs: collect_clip_audio`) puts `EngineSnapshot::clip_audio: (ClipId, take) → ClipAudio { data, start_frame }` into the snapshot for every sounding take of a clip with warp or pitch (`Clip::sounding_takes`: the active one or the comp takes): the finished audio, and while new audio is being computed — the previous audio of the same take of the clip (not silence); if no processing is needed — the sample itself. `start_frame` is the audio frame at the start of the clip. The job is `ProcessJob::for_clip(clip, take, &TempoMap, rate)`. A tempo change is a project edit: the snapshot and the new computation follow automatically; a finished background computation does not publish a snapshot, so it is noticed by the watcher `daw-bootstrap/src/clip_audio_watch.rs` (a ~20 Hz thread): `finished` grew → `ProjectService::refresh_clip_audio()` (only if such clips exist). Export (`daw-render`) finishes computing the audio via `compute` and on failure refuses with a clear error.

**Warping across tempo changes.** If the tempo changes inside a warped clip (there is a point with a different tempo, or the clip starts inside a ramp), there is no single stretch: the job carries a curve `StretchAnchor { source, output }` (source audio frame → processed frame from the clip start): a beat of the audio lands on the tick where the source tempo puts it, and the time of that tick is taken from the map. Points — the start, every tempo point inside, a 120-tick step inside ramps, the end; linear in between. Only the clip's piece is processed (`start_frame` = 0). `daw_stretch::process_curve` is the same WSOLA, but the "expected" position of the input window is taken from the curve (`wsola::stretch_mapped`; a constant stretch is a special case of it, so a clip without a tempo change inside sounds exactly as before, and its jobs are unchanged). The cache key is the points themselves: they depend only on the tempo points inside the clip and the tempo at its start, and on the clip's position, offset, and source tempo, so a tempo change outside the clip recomputes nothing, while moving or trimming a clip with a tempo change inside does recompute. Each piece of the curve stays within 0.25…4. Tests: `daw-stretch/src/tests.rs` (click beats after a step and inside a ramp land where the map puts them; a straight curve = a single stretch), `daw-contracts/src/warp.rs`, `daw-project/src/tests/warp.rs`.

**Engine** (`daw-engine/src/audio_voices.rs`): a clip with warp or pitch (`Clip::has_sound_processing`) plays only from `clip_audio` (no entry — it stays silent rather than playing the file out of time); the position in the audio = `start_frame` (for a sample — `offset_frames`, for a single stretch — `offset_frames × stretch`, for a curve — 0) + frames from the clip start along the tempo map. If the clip's audio in a new snapshot is different (recomputed for a new tempo), the playing voice switches to it at the same clip position, and the old audio goes to the release queue (like everything in the audio thread).

**Crossfades.** Audio clips on the same audio track may overlap at their edges: at most two at any point, a clip does not lie inside another and does not start on the same tick as it (`clip_rules.rs: check_track`, on the resulting state; note clips still do not overlap). An overlap plays as an equal-power crossfade the length of the overlap (`Clip::crossfades` gives the fade-in and fade-out lengths; the voice multiplies by `sin` of the overlap fraction on top of the regular fades): these are two ordinary voices, with no allocations. The frontend repeats the placement rules in `domain/clips.ts` (`trackPlacementProblem`, `canPlace`, `crossfades`; used by sample drop `domain/dropSample.ts`; the mock `mock/clipRules.ts` checks the same rules with the backend's codes), and the timeline draws an overlap as shading with an "X" (`timelineRender.ts: drawCrossfade`). Drag an audio clip onto the edge of another and you get a crossfade.

**UI.** A selected audio clip opens `ui/pianoroll/AudioClipPanel.tsx` in the clip panel: "Warp to tempo" (`actions.clips.setWarp`), "Source tempo" (`setSourceBpm`), "Pitch, semitones" (`setPitch`); the clip label on the timeline is "⟲ 128", "↕ +3" (`domain/warp.ts: warpMarks`), and the waveform reads the audio at the clip's tempo (`clipTempo`). Pure frontend rules (tempo guessing, length recalculation) are in `domain/warp.ts`; the mock applies them without real DSP (lengths only). For similar clip audio processing (reverse, normalization): a field in `Clip` → a condition in `ProcessJob::for_clip`/`Clip::has_sound_processing` → computation in `daw_stretch::process` → an edit rule next to `warp_rules.rs` → a field in the panel.

**Limitations.** WSOLA has no separate transient handling: heavy stretching smears hits; frame resampling is cubic, not polyphase; while audio is being computed after a tempo change, the clip briefly plays the previous computation; the time signature when guessing by length is always 4/4.
